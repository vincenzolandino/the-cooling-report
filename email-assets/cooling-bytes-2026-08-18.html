Infrastructure 8 GW PORTS-Pike Technology Campus, Pike County, Ohio  ·  4.25GW phase one behind 9.2GW of on-site gas

NVIDIA Put $1.5 Billion Into SB Energy and Backed $105 Billion of Lease. The Campus It Underwrites Has to Reject the Heat of 8 Gigawatts.

Three thousand seven hundred acres of federal land at the former Portsmouth Gaseous Diffusion Plant, a Cold War uranium enrichment site that stopped running in 2001, developed by SoftBank's SB Energy under a Department of Energy partnership, leased by OpenAI for twenty years, with NVIDIA as exclusive compute supplier.

2.3M Tons of refrigeration at full buildout, roughly 27 billion BTU per hour
1.15 Implied PUE if 9.2GW of generation is sized against 8GW of IT
0 Cooling numbers published: no supply temperature, no water balance

A PUE near 1.15 across 2.3 million tons does not close with mechanical chillers running through an Ohio summer. It closes with liquid at the rack, a high supply temperature, and dry coolers doing the rejection most of the year, which is NVIDIA's own reference design. Gaseous diffusion was among the most electricity-intensive industrial processes ever run at scale, so the site came with power and water already permitted. Whether any of that cooling infrastructure survived decommissioning is the first question worth asking, and no filing answers it.

The Mechanical Contractor That Turned a Factory Into Colossus in 123 Days Has Filed $137 Million in Liens. It Owes Its Own Subcontractors $200 Million.

Darana Hybrid handled mechanical contracting at xAI's Colossus and Colossus II in Memphis from 2024 through June 2026, roughly $1.5 billion of work. An electro-mechanical contractor is the firm that puts in the power distribution, the wiring, and the cooling. Owner Darryl Cuttell says payments stopped in April after the xAI and SpaceX merger, and puts the total owed at "probably north of $600 million." None of it has been tested in court.

$118M Lien against the Tulane Road site, plus $18M at Paul Lowry Road
$400M Further liens planned against the Southaven, Mississippi plant
$2.2M Separate lien filed against the water treatment plant on site

The smallest number there is the one to read twice. A water treatment plant conditions cooling makeup and handles blowdown, and it decides whether an operator runs its towers on anything other than potable supply. Colossus already draws on the order of 812,000 gallons a day against the Memphis Sand aquifer. Somebody financed a 123-day schedule, and it was the contractor carrying payroll and subcontractor invoices against progress billings that stopped arriving.

Elko Locked Ten Years of Reclaimed Water to a Golf Course and a Ranch. The Vote Was About a Data Center Nobody Has Proposed.

The city council voted unanimously on August 11 for a ten-year Water Rights Priority Agreement with Bar L Ranch, Inc., prioritizing category B reclaimed water for golf course and ranch irrigation. Wastewater Superintendent Scott Dancz said the city can shut off access if the water goes anywhere else. City Attorney David Stanton said it binds future owners of the property. No data center had applied.

"The golf course won."

The Cooling Report  ·  August 17, 2026

Reclaimed effluent is the answer this industry already agreed to give. Amazon committed to a wastewater recovery system and pipeline for Gilroy. Veolia supplies Amazon reclaimed water in Mississippi. The EPA's reuse action plan names data center cooling directly. Elko showed the failure mode in a single meeting.

Municipal effluent is a finite asset with existing claimants, and a council can contract it away before an operator ever files. That leaves three options in northern Nevada: potable supply, which is finished before the first hearing, groundwater in an allocated basin, or reject to air and pay for it in fan power.

Energy

Google and Amazon Turned Into Two of the World's Biggest Battery Buyers. Nothing in the Announcements Says What Cools the Batteries.

950 TWh by 2030 IEA projection for global data center electricity, from 485 TWh in 2025
25 GW of Batteries Potentially installed at data centers by the end of the decade
30 GWh, One Site Google's iron-air battery at Pine Island, Minnesota, rated near 100 hours

Lithium iron phosphate holds cycle life best in roughly the 15C to 35C band. The racks are moving the other way, toward 45C supply water, precisely so their heat becomes easy to reject. A gigawatt-hour of storage is therefore a second cooling system with its own setpoint, its own fire code, and its own containment, and it cannot share the warm loop the IT side is standardizing on.

Peak solar and peak dry bulb arrive within a couple of hours of each other, which is the worst moment for a dry cooler and the cheapest power on the site. Overnight the ambient drops, the dry cooler recovers its approach, and every kilowatt the mechanical plant draws comes out of a battery somebody had to buy, site, and cool. Shifting deferrable work into the cool hours wins twice. Almost nobody schedules compute that way.
