NVIDIA Says 45C Coolant Saves a 50MW Site $4 Million a Year. The Citation Behind the Math Measures Air.

Josh Parker, NVIDIA's head of sustainability, published the figure on the company's own blog on June 21. The post says raising chiller plant temperatures by one degree cuts cooling energy costs about 4 percent, and hangs "industry estimates" on a link to an Energy Star page. That page says something else. It puts the saving at 4 to 5 percent per degree Fahrenheit of increase in server inlet air temperature, inside a band drawn from ASHRAE's 2008 guidance.

1.8x A Celsius degree against the Fahrenheit the source measures
0 Fans in Rubin, the platform the heuristic is applied to
~88 hrs Chiller runtime a year under NVIDIA's own 1% figure

Two substitutions happen between the source and the sentence. Server inlet air becomes chiller plant. Fahrenheit becomes an unspecified degree in a post where every other temperature is Celsius. The Energy Star page also warns that pushing inlet air too warm makes server fans spin up and claw the savings back, and Rubin has no fans. Ali Heydari, NVIDIA's director of data center cooling and infrastructure, drew the other boundary himself: the dry-cooler loop runs without evaporative cooling except "maybe 1% of the year when we might need chillers." Read that as an equipment list. The chillers stay in the building, bought and commissioned and maintained, to carry the full load for roughly 88 hours a year.

LG Cleared NVIDIA AI Factory Validation on a 600 kW CDU. It Is the Smallest Unit on Its Own Roadmap.

Every liquid-cooled rack NVIDIA ships has to hand its heat to something, and that something is a coolant distribution unit. NVIDIA decides which CDUs qualify. Few clear the bar. LG got one through on July 27, the first liquid cooling equipment from a Korean manufacturer to pass the program.

1.4 MW The unit named when LG was chasing certification
600 kW The unit that actually cleared validation
1 / 2.5 / 4 MW Roadmap tiers above it, none of them 1.4

The gap is the story. When we covered LG pulling its chiller target forward, the CDU in play was a 1.4 MW unit. The one that cleared is 600 kW, and the published roadmap climbing above it reads 1 MW, 2.5 MW, 4 MW, with no 1.4 entry anywhere on it. Validation is per-unit, not per-vendor. An operator specifying LG at the top of that roadmap is buying a plan, and only the bottom rung has NVIDIA's signature on it so far.

Water

The Kansas Health Institute Says US Data Center Water Use Could Quadruple by 2028. The Gap Between Double and Quadruple Is a Cooling Design Decision.

Emma Uridge and Jasmin Kamruddin put a range on the water question, and the range is wide enough to be a planning problem. US data centers consumed an estimated 17 billion gallons directly in 2023. That draw could double or quadruple by 2028. A 2x-to-4x spread is not a forecast so much as an admission that the answer depends on choices operators have not made yet, and the largest of those choices is how the heat leaves the building.

"Water Usage Effectiveness measures the property line. The largest share of the draw is at a power plant that never appears on the site permit."

The Cooling Report  ·  July 27, 2026

KHI puts cooling at as much as 40 percent of total electricity draw, splitting the load three ways: computing 40 percent, cooling 40 percent, other IT 20 percent. Average rack density runs 36 kW in 2023 to 50 kW by 2027, with some designs reaching 600 kW. One in five US data centers already sat in a water-stressed area in 2021, before this buildout landed.

Closed-loop and dry cooling cut onsite consumption hard, then hand the difference back as fan and compressor power. Fossil generation supplies 56 percent of the electricity feeding these sites, and thermoelectric plants consume water to make it. That is the mechanism behind the finding that most of the draw never shows up on the site meter at all.

Water

Google Will Air-Cool the First of Three Botetourt Buildings. Carvins Cove Is 14.6 Feet Down and the Contract Still Reads 8 Million Gallons.

14.6 Feet Below Full Carvins Cove in early July, 65.9 percent of capacity
22 Years Since the water authority last ran its drought contingency plan
8M Gallons What the existing water contract still allows, unchanged

The Roanoke Valley sits in D2 severe drought. On July 9 the Western Virginia Water Authority moved every customer to Stage 1 voluntary conservation, the first time in 22 years of service it has run that plan. Two weeks later Google told Botetourt County the first of three data center buildings would be air-cooled.

Air cooling is the right answer to a drought, and it is also the answer that moves the load onto the grid at exactly the hours the grid is worst positioned to carry it. The commitment covers one of three buildings. Nothing in the announcement amends the water contract underneath the site, which still reads 8 million gallons. A cooling method announced is not a contract renegotiated, and only one of those two things is enforceable.
