Amazon Is Backing 7.65 Gigawatts of Gas in Pecos County. Everything It Powers Still Has to Reject Heat Into a West Texas Summer.

It would be the largest single emissions source in the US power fleet. Going off-grid means the campus builds its own generation and its own heat rejection, and only one of those two is a public filing.

West Texas hands an operator one gift, a low coincident wet bulb, so a tower gets a colder approach and does more work per gallon. The gallon is the problem, because Permian makeup water is already drawn against by oil and gas, agriculture, and municipalities. Seven gigawatts of turbines carry their own cooling bill on top: inlet air cooling to hold capacity through an August afternoon, lube oil and generator coolers, and a steam condenser with its own tower if any of it runs combined cycle. The emissions figure is public. The water balance for the generation half of this campus is not.

An NVIDIA Distinguished Engineer Says the Goal Is a Data Center Nobody Notices. His Number for It Is 113 Degree Coolant.

Sean James started as a submarine electrician in the Navy and spent more than 25 years building data centers at Microsoft before joining NVIDIA. His line: "Data centers need to fade into the background too, and that is the goal of every data center designer." At 113F facility supply, which is 45C, the outdoor loop rejects through dry coolers and outside air across most climates for most of the year. The tower comes off the site plan and the makeup rate goes to zero.

3 to 5 PUE in the 1990s, against 1.2 to 1.5 today, with no political credit for it
1,000 MW Load dropping off the grid in seconds, enough to trigger a grid alert
45C Supply temperature that removes the tower, the basin, and the blowdown permit

What leaves with the tower: the biocide program, the drift eliminators, the scale chemistry, the discharge permit, and the plume that shows up in every community hearing. Every other story in this issue is a fight about one of those. The constraint is the installed fleet, because a hall built for 20C supply water needs new coils, new pumps, and a new outdoor loop to reach 45C, and nobody is budgeting that retrofit yet.

The Buildout Needs 550,000 More Plumbers and 500,000 More Electricians. Mechanical Pipefitters Are the People Who Actually Install Liquid Cooling.

Meeting the deadlines on projects already proposed would take roughly 500,000 more electricians, 300,000 more welders, and 550,000 more plumbers, with electricians, HVAC technicians and mechanical pipefitters the hardest roles to fill. Contractors are turning away work or poaching crews from smaller firms. Reported wages for AI data center electricians under 30 run in the $240,000 to $280,000 range, from industry reporting rather than a federal series, so treat the bands as directional.

"Two of the three scarce trades are the liquid cooling install."

A direct-to-chip deployment is pipefitting end to end: secondary loop headers, rack manifolds, CDU tie-ins, hydrostatic testing, flushing to a particulate spec, glycol charging, and commissioning the flow balance across a row. Same craft and same certifications as the chilled water plant it hangs off.

Crews get pulled to the electrical critical path first, so mechanical trim and loop commissioning are where a thin crew shows up as slip. Ask any vendor pitching a liquid platform how many labor hours per rack their system takes, and how many of those hours need a journeyperson card. Most have never been asked.

74 Percent of Pennsylvania Voters Oppose a Data Center in Their Town. The State Now Makes Anything Over 10 Megawatts Report Its Water.

74% Opposed Quinnipiac poll of 895 Pennsylvania voters, against 16 percent in favor
41k Members A resistance group that had 500 members in March, adding 1,000 a week at peak
10 MW Reporting Floor Annual energy and water filings now required above one AI hall

Thirty data center bills this session, eight in May and eight more in June. Senator Katie Muth's office logged 504 emails about data centers, 182 of them mentioning electricity bills. East Whiteland Township in Chester County is the current flashpoint and will not be the last.

An operator reporting once is a press release. An operator reporting every year builds a series, and a series is what a supervisor holds up at the second hearing. Evaporative consumption tracks summer wet bulb, so year two after a hot August looks worse than year one, and the operator has to explain the shape of the curve rather than the size of one figure. That is the argument closed-loop designs win on paper before anyone breaks ground.
